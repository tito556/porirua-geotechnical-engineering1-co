Porirua's geology catches people out. The basin sits on a mix of greywacke bedrock, ancient alluvial gravels, and soft Holocene marine sediments. What looks like solid ground from the surface often hides buried paleochannels, perched water tables, or compressible silt lenses that conventional boreholes miss. Electrical resistivity testing lets us map these contrasts before the excavator arrives. In our experience working across Whitby, Titahi Bay, and the city centre, a well-executed Vertical Electrical Sounding (VES) survey reveals the vertical layering that governs settlement behaviour. Combine it with CPT testing when you need continuous tip resistance and pore pressure data alongside the geoelectric profile, or add MASW to correlate resistivity boundaries with shear-wave velocity for seismic site class determination under NZS 1170.5. Porirua's shallow groundwater and tidal influence near Te Awarua-o-Porirua Harbour make resistivity a practical first-pass investigation tool.

In Porirua's coastal basin, a VES survey often reveals a hidden soft layer at 4-8 metres depth that standard residential investigations overlook.

Approach and scope

The most common mistake we see in Porirua is relying solely on sparse boreholes in areas with lens-shaped soft deposits. A single SPT refusal on greywacke at one corner of the site doesn't mean the entire footprint is competent. VES fills the gaps. We run Schlumberger arrays with expanding electrode spacing to penetrate 20 to 60 metres depth, measuring apparent resistivity as current lines intersect different materials. Water-saturated clays read low (5–30 Ωm), dry gravels read high (200–1000+ Ωm), and weathered greywacke sits somewhere in between. Our team processes the field curves with 1D inversion software, constrained by local borehole logs when available. Porirua's wind can be brutal on cable layouts, so we schedule surveying early morning when conditions are calmer. For roading projects along State Highway 1 or foundation investigations near the railway corridor, resistivity profiles help engineers decide where to place additional test pits or deeper SPT drilling without wasting budget on redundant investigation points.

Site-specific factors

NZS 4404:2010 for subdivision earthworks expects developers to understand subsurface conditions before construction begins. Porirua City Council also requires adequate investigation for building consent on sites with known soft ground potential. The hazard here is differential settlement. A shallow foundation spanning from a buried greywacke ridge into an old stream channel filled with estuarine silt will crack within the first few years. Resistivity contrasts between these materials are sharp, and VES profiles make the transition visible. We've surveyed sites in eastern Porirua where a 2-metre drop in bedrock across 15 horizontal metres was invisible from the surface but obvious in the resistivity data. Ignoring this layering means expensive remedial underpinning later. Liquefaction assessment under the MBIE guidelines also benefits from resistivity data: saturated sandy layers with low resistivity can be targeted for liquefaction analysis using SPT or CPT, rather than assuming homogeneous soil conditions across the entire lot. Porirua's seismic hazard is real, and ground characterisation is the first step in managing it.

Related technical services

01

Vertical Electrical Sounding (VES)

1D resistivity depth profiling using Schlumberger array with expanding electrode spacing. Ideal for determining bedrock depth, identifying water tables, and mapping soft sediment thickness at discrete locations across a site.

02

2D Electrical Resistivity Tomography (ERT)

Multi-electrode resistivity imaging along a line, producing continuous cross-sections. We use this for paleochannel delineation, fault zone detection, and tracking groundwater salinity changes near the Porirua Harbour margin.

03

Combined geophysical site assessment

Integrated VES and MASW surveys to jointly interpret resistivity and shear-wave velocity. This dual-parameter approach strengthens seismic site classification and reduces the ambiguity inherent in any single geophysical method.

Quick answers

What depth can a VES survey reach in Porirua's geology?

With the Schlumberger array and a maximum current electrode spacing (AB/2) of 150 metres, we typically resolve layers down to 50 or 60 metres. The actual penetration depends on subsurface resistivity. Conductive clays limit depth more than resistive gravels or greywacke. We always run a preliminary site check to estimate achievable penetration before mobilising.

How much does an electrical resistivity survey cost for a residential section in Porirua?

For a typical residential site requiring three to five VES soundings with interpretation, costs range from NZ$1,080 to NZ$1,930 depending on access, terrain, and whether 2D resistivity lines are included. Steep slopes, dense bush, or sites requiring helicopter support fall outside this range. We provide a fixed-price proposal after reviewing the site location and investigation objectives.

Can resistivity testing replace boreholes for foundation design?

No, and we never recommend relying on resistivity alone. Geophysics gives you continuous spatial coverage but geotechnical parameters like SPT N-values, undrained shear strength, or moisture content require direct sampling. Resistivity guides where to drill and helps interpolate between boreholes, but a defensible foundation design in Porirua always combines both methods.
